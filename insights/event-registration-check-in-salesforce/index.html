Event registration and check-in inside Salesforce, without another tool

Run a customer event from Salesforce: a branded registration page, an instant confirmation with a calendar invite, QR check-in at the door, and a feedback survey to everyone who came.

SalesHub · November 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Customer events are where relationships get built, and where the data usually gets lost. Registrations sit in an event tool, the door list is a spreadsheet, and the follow-up survey goes out a week late, if at all. Run the whole thing from Salesforce and every step lands on the people you already know.

The four moments of an event

Every event, from a roundtable of twelve to a user conference, has the same four moments. Get each one right and you know exactly who registered, who came, and what they thought.

MomentWhat the guest seesWhat you get in Salesforce
RegisterA branded page with the date, time and placeThe registrant, matched to a Contact and Account
ConfirmA confirmation code and add-to-calendar buttonsA record of who registered and when
Check inA QR code at the door, or a quick name searchWho actually came, with the time
Follow upA short thank-you survey after the eventEvent NPS and comments on each attendee

Step 1. A registration page that looks like you

Use your logo, your colours and a clear event card: the name, the date and time in the guest's time zone, the place, and one sentence on why it's worth their time. Ask only what you need: name, email and company are usually enough. Every extra field costs registrations.

Step 2. Confirm instantly, with a calendar invite

The confirmation screen is the moment a registration turns into attendance. Show a confirmation code and give one-click buttons to add the event to Google Calendar or Outlook, or download a calendar file. Guests who put it on their calendar are far more likely to show up.

Step 3. Check people in at the door

Print one QR code at the entrance. Guests scan it and check themselves in with their email, or your team searches the guest list on a phone or tablet and taps each name. Either way, you finish the event knowing exactly who came, not just who registered.

Step 4. Thank attendees and ask how it went

Send the thank-you survey the same day, only to people who checked in. Ask one question that matters ("How likely are you to recommend this event to a colleague?") and one open question ("What should we do differently next time?"). A low score from a key account is worth a call, not just a line in a report.

Why it matters more inside Salesforce

  • Account teams see it. An account owner can see that three people from their customer came to the event, and what they thought.
  • No list cleanup. Registrants match to existing Contacts, so there's no export and import after every event.
  • Follow-up has an owner. An unhappy attendee gets the same follow-up as any unhappy customer.
