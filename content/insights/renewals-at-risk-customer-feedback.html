Customers rarely cancel without warning. They tell you months earlier, in a survey comment, a low score after a support case, or by going quiet. The warning is usually already in your data. What's missing is a way to see it next to the renewal date and the money at stake.

Three signals that predict churn

  • Negative feedback on things that matter. A low score that mentions price, reliability or support is a stronger signal than one about a minor feature.
  • Silence. An account that hasn't answered a survey in six months isn't happy by default. It may have stopped caring.
  • A key person unhappy. One detractor who is the decision maker outweighs five happy end users.

Step 1. Put feedback and renewals in one view

Feedback lives in surveys. Renewal dates and contract value live on the account or opportunity. Bring them together on the Account so each one has three facts: how they feel, when they renew, and how much is at stake. In Salesforce, those facts are already a click apart; the work is showing them side by side.

Step 2. Sort accounts into a few clear groups

GroupWhat it meansNext step
Act nowRecent negative feedback and a renewal coming upPlan the save this week: an owner, a call, a fix
WatchMixed signals, or negative feedback with a later renewalA check-in before it turns into Act now
HealthyRecent positive feedbackAsk for a referral, a review or an expansion
Not heard fromNo feedback in monthsSend a survey, or have the account owner call

Step 3. Rank by money and time, then keep the list short

Within Act now, rank by contract value and by how soon the renewal is. Then cap the weekly list at a handful of accounts. A list of forty accounts gets ignored; a list of five with one clear next step each gets worked.

Step 4. Give every at-risk account a save plan

A save plan is simple: who owns it, what you heard, what you'll do, and by when. Write it on the account so the whole team sees it. After the call, record what happened. Over a few months, you'll learn which saves work.

Step 5. Don't forget the healthy accounts

Revenue protection isn't only about saving unhappy customers. Happy accounts with a renewal coming are your best moment to ask for a referral, a case study or an expansion. The same view that finds risk finds growth.

What to measure

  • Revenue renewing in the next 120 days, split by group
  • At-risk accounts with a save plan, and how many were saved
  • Accounts with no feedback in six months, and how many you reached
